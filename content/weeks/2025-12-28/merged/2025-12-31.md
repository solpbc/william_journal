### Page

The account of this year, 1595.

In the coffer at the year's beginning: £24 10s.

Received.
Of the company, my share of the weeks played: £51 14s.
Of my Lord Chamberlain at Richmond, for hose, my part: 10s.
Of the Queen's warrant of March, my part: £1 17s 6d.
Of Sir Edward Hoby's reward, my part: 12s 6d.
Of Richard Quiney, in part of his £2: £1.
Together: £55 14s.

Laid out.
To Mistress Ansell, four quarters: £4.
Firing and washing, the winter weeks: 12s 6d.
Board and victuals in London: £12 14s 4d.
Sent home: the Lent quarter by my own hand, 18s of it to Anne; three quarters by the carrier at 60s; to Anne in August, over the quarter's, 30s: £11 8s.
My father's small debts, paid in Lent (42s of it out of the Lent quarter's 60s): £5.
Toward the barn at Stratford: £1.
To the brief for the fire: £2.
Lent to Richard Quiney: £2.
The journeys: the horse in Lent, the road in summer: £2 18s.
Books: Brooke, Daniel, the Plutarch, Southwell: 17s.
Paper, ink and lights: £1 4s.
Apparel, shoes and mending: £1 10s.
Gifts to the children, and the Christmas pack: £1 4s.
The tooth, and the cloves before it: 1s 2d.
Church and the dole: 5s.
Together: £46 14s.

In the coffer at the year's end: £33 10s, counted twice.

Expected, not received: my part of the three nights at Richmond. Not entered. The Queen's debt is better than another man's coin, says my lord; John has written it in his book and looks at it.

Richard Quiney yet owes £1.

Field's Holinshed: still here.

The play of the wood: go on with the workmen. Do not make them fools before they have had leave to be men.
