### Page

Two sheets of Field's crown paper, folded down to small leaves. No name. No number. No superscription.

Since first mine eye tooke notice of a face,
The yeere hath turnd his coat, and turnd again.
The leafe that held is litter in the place,
And what was greene is browner with the raine.
I doe not write a name. The season can.
It goes, and comes, and goes, and leaues the bough.
A man may number falling, if he can,
And still not say what he is numbering now.
The haruest came. It was not mine to keepe.
The morning is more cold then yesterday.
I set this downe for any eye to reade,
And put no superscription on the leafe.
Let the yeere speake. The yeere hath no cause to lie.
I haue a hand. The rest I will not say.

I know the better course, and take the worse.
The doore is shut. I am the one who shut it.
What I decide at morning I reherse,
And by the afternoone I haue vndone it.
Desire is a poore clerke and keepes no booke.
He writes, and sands, and writes the line againe.
I tell him he was answered, and I looke,
And he is at the table with his pen.
So. Let him write. I will not signe my name.
The leafe may lie vpon its face, and hide.
I am not bound to read it, nor to blame
The hand that made it, which is mine, and tyred.
Enough. The better course can wait till day.
I know it. Turn the paper. Put it away.

The second leaf, turned face down. The first left as it is.
