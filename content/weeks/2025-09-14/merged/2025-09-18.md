## Pulse

- title: Sweet Shakspeare
- one sentence: At Paul's you read two words in a margin, shut the book gently, and at the Jackdaw told Burbage low enough that Kemp stopped eating.
- details: At ten, at the sign of the White Greyhound, Field put the book in your hands and Harrison found the leaf with a stubby thumb. Out in the margin, small, two words: Sweet Shakspeare. You read them, and the names above and below, and you read them again. The rest of the leaf was too small to take. No man had put your name in a printed book before to say you were good. Three years ago a dying man put you in print as a crow in borrowed feathers. This one calls you sweet, and you could not decide which was harder to stand still under. Harrison said a Cambridge gentleman calls you sweet, that he had told two customers, and that the Lucrece is respected. Field, low, said it was for the poems, not for a king in Shoreditch and not for a boy under a wall, and that you are wasted on players. You shut the book very gently and did not buy it. You have it by heart. Going into Cheapside you said it was sweet like a pippin, like a child's physic, from a man in Cambridge you never saw, and you laughed with your shoulders as far as the Stocks. In the afternoon the Knave was up, and you were not cast. You stood at the tiring-house door. The plot gave no name of a part. At the Jackdaw you could not keep from telling Burbage, low. Kemp was on the next bench, eating. He stopped eating, and he tried the name once.
