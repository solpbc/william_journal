## Pulse

- title: Heard a pavan and set a cross
- one sentence: You stood three pews from the Mayor, ate a penny loaf a thumb short, and at evening heard a pavan through an open shutter and set a cross.
- details: At St Helen's the Mayor was in his pew, furred and exact, three pews off. He gave the litany its answers short and cold. Neither of you looked. The north aisle was full again with the households that went out of the City in July. Among them was a dark head under a plain hood, and it did not turn. You learned nothing from the sermon. The jaw had a pulse in it. At dinner the loaf was a penny and a full thumb short of the notch. The back was the same back. You gave the table no story. At evening you walked for the sake of the back, through the lane off Bishopsgate Street. The shutter was open. A pavan was taken slowly, with a hard place in the left hand, and then the lid shut. You stood no longer than a man might to get a stone out of his shoe. In the close you said the three sentences. In the almanac, against the fourteenth, a small cross and no word, the first since June. Nothing else was written. You let the jaw be the reason.
