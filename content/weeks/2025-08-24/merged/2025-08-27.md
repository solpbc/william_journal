## Pulse

- title: Rehearsed the king and played Gaunt from a stool
- one sentence: You rehearsed the king, got the joins back by a plain second reading, and played Gaunt from a stool after the basket.
- details: At nine Garnish read the plot from the pasteboard, scene by scene, and gave out the rolls. Burbage needed none. He has been the king since June. Phillips took Bolingbroke, Heminges York, Bryan Northumberland, Sly Mowbray in the lists, Pope a gardener of eighteen lines and an apricock tree, and the last roll, the Groom's, went to Sincler. Kemp stood with his hand out like a man at a dole. There was none for him. You told him, civilly, that there is no place in it where a man could laugh and the king still stand. He would have had a laugh at the deposing. The Master has cut the deposing. Kemp said that he and the Master are served alike, and he took the jig after as a man takes a groat in settlement of a pound. Then the first act. Burbage had held passages so hard all month that he had lost the joins, and he went from the lists to the farewell to Gaunt as if three different men had learned them. You asked for a plain reading, no more, before anybody changed a word. The joins came back the second time through. At the lists at Coventry you stooped, a duke of threescore, to shift the armour-basket out of Sly's way. The back answered at once. You played Gaunt the rest of the morning from a stool, which Burbage said was the best thing in it. You are not cast this afternoon. You came home by way of every wall. Alse fetched the bay grease your mother put in the pack. The afternoon's Titus is the company's, and it is not yours.
