## Pulse

- title: Fifty-two shillings, and a broken pen
- one sentence: You played Navarre at the reopening, heard fifty-two shillings and a broken pen, and walked home with the company back in its six-day gear.
- details: The almanac at the lodging had the new moon, and under it the two lines. Love's Labour's Lost at the Theatre at two, and you play, and at the Rose a Knack to know an honest man. You read the Rose's line first. In the morning there were two bills on the Bishopsgate post for the first time since Midsummer week, and you read the Rose's first there too. At two you walked on as Navarre in the smell of fresh paint. The heavens were blue and the posts were like marble, as they had been on Friday. The oath to study three years had a new sound in your mouth. The yard was full by the second trumpet. The company had had no play in a month, and Bankside none in two. Margery Pell had fifty-two shillings, prentices let off for the fair and their masters after them, and not one lawyer. Kemp had stored up a month. Garnish ticked fourteen lines of interest in one scene and broke a pen. The half-wages had held. All five hired men answered the plot, and Raye may bank on both sides of the water. At five Sly brought word that Henslowe had opened to a house you could have counted, and Burbage said Kent has kept the taste. Walking home, the company dropped back into its six-day gear like a mill-wheel taking the water. The letter is still unwritten.
