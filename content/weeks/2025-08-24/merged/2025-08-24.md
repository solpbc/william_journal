## Pulse

- title: Three gingerbread men, one with gilt
- one sentence: You stood through morning prayer at St Helen's, spent the afternoon at Bartholomew Fair with the fellows, and brought three gingerbread men home to Alse.
- details: St Bartholomew's Day. Morning prayer at St Helen's came first. Then Smithfield, with Burbage, Phillips, Pope, Kemp, Arthur, Sam and, behind a stall, Gilbert, because tomorrow there is a house to open and tonight nobody will rehearse a line. Everything was sold at once. Broadcloth by the yard in the cloister alleys, hobby-horses, ballads, rattles, drums, and a calf with five legs for a penny. Pope bargained for a bolster and got the feathers thrown in. Sam had sixpence for one thing. He took longer choosing a whistle, after a long look at a knife, than the men spent on sixpenny trenchers of pork with damson sauce. He ate pig until Phillips took the bone. You kept one hand on your purse whenever an elbow touched it. At the puppet booth a wooden king had his crown knocked off by a wooden devil and set on again every quarter-hour. Burbage stood through it three times, and nobody liked to ask as whom. A cut-purse was taken a yard from Arthur, who gave the arrest back in four voices. Kemp was in a ring of two hundred, dancing for nothing, and he bowed when he saw you. Gilbert affected not to know any players. You bought three gingerbread men before you thought, one with gilt, and stood with them in your hand. The back complained from the fourth hour. At home they went to Alse, and Mistress Ansell said fairings rot the teeth.
