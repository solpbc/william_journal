## Pulse

- title: Bound short ears at Hewlands
- one sentence: You bound at Hewlands from six till four, and at the table you kept your lord's ear for plays.
- details: The shoe went on before six. At Hewlands the ears were short and the stooks stood far apart, and you bound behind the sickles with an hour for dinner when Bartholomew called it. You stopped at four. On the way back, at the end of the burned row, two men had their hands in a bushel of thin barley at a malt-house door, and you did not go up the row. Richard Quiney came to the house, with Alderman Sturley, because you had not gone to the shop. The matter was barley. You said the maltsters should be made to sell. Sturley asked which price you had paid, for what, and where, and you had paid none. Anne gave the town's side and did not rescue you. Quiney said two-and-twenty shillings the quarter, and thirty by Christmas, and your father enjoyed him. You said you would give what you can give, and that your lord's ear is kept for plays. Two gentlemen of the county were left unnamed, for one brief covering both fires.
