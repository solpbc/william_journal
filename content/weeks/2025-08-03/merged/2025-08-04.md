## Pulse

- title: Two leaves, and the shoe stays off
- one sentence: With the shoe still off, you wrote two fair leaves of the winter list.
- details: Your feet still would not take a shoe, so Anne set you down at eight with the household list for the winter, written fair, before you were allowed to open the play. Salt became two lines. Candles and rush-lights became three. You wrote four bushels of malt, and she struck them to two, and did not say she was right. Soap was the old quarrel with Joan, and it went on the page with her name. Your mother corrected an old field-name you had spelled as Londoners say it, and you put the missing letter back without being told the ground's name a second way. By ten the list was two leaves. In the afternoon word came that Bartholomew's sickles start at six, and the old shoes went on over doubled hose and came off again until morning. The bundle stayed tied.
