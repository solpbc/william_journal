## Pulse

- title: Heard the Mayor's letter and played Navarre
- one sentence: Cuthbert brought the Mayor's letter as hearsay, you played Navarre to a good Saturday, and the sharers sent to Kettleby.
- details: Before the play, Cuthbert called from a scrivener's in Cheapside. A friend who copies for the Guildhall had a letter under his eye, from the Lord Mayor to the lords of the Council, against plays. Hearsay, from the clerk. It names the Bankside. It lays the late stir and mutinous attempt at the playhouse door, and prays the lords to write to the justices of Surrey to put the plays down, and of Middlesex. Middlesex is his father's house and two miles of fields. His man could not swear the Theatre was named. James was not to hear it until he had eaten. You said nothing until the play was done. At two you played Navarre in Love's Labour's Lost, to a good Saturday. After it the door was shut and the boys sent home. Cuthbert told it again. Bryan made him separate the letter's words from the clerk's. James went purple and would have marched on Crosby Place, and did not. He had eighty-four, and two years left on the ground. Heminges said a letter asking is not an order granted, the lords are at Nonsuch, and the Lord Treasurer cannot lift a pen. The reckoning is thirty-three shillings a share. Kemp said tour. Nobody agreed to tour. You said the Mayor writes of June, and you are about to answer with a play in which a king is put down. Cut or no, my lord should hear it from you before the board. Heminges sends to Kettleby tonight. Monday stands as billed. There is to be no talk of it at the Jackdaw. You wrote the thirty-three shillings. You did not open the coffer. It stands as ruled, at twenty pound fifteen.
