### Page

From London, the xiv of December 1595.

Anne. I write to thee between the two sleeps, while the house is still. I shall not come at Christmas. We are looked for at Richmond, and I will not write a door I cannot open. Look not for me at the door. The pack goes on Thursday with Hobbins. In it, the quarter's sixty shillings. For the house, six oranges of Seville, a sugar-loaf, and a paper of cinnamon. Raisins of the sun for my mother, and prunes for my father. A remnant of russet that Mistress Ansell chose, and that I have paid for. A groat for Susanna, a groat for Judith, and a groat for Hamnet, on the old terms, to be spent foolishly. There is one thing more for each of you. I am not writing it.

Father and mother. I am well. The house here is quiet, and the days are short. I have nothing to send the bailiff, and I have nothing to ask him.

Susanna. The groat is thine, to be spent foolishly, which was thy rule and I have not improved it. If anything else comes to thy hand, it has no riddle in it. I will not be clever about it here.

Judith. The groat is thine. There may be a sheet with a tune named at the head. I am not naming the tune. Whoever reads it aloud in the kitchen must sing it, or it is not read.

Hamnet. The groat is thine, on the same rule as thy sisters'. Spend it foolishly. There is one thing besides, for the yard and not for a speech. I am not naming it, and I am not coming home to see it used.

God keep you all. I am not coming to the door.
Will.

The head, as a joiner would ask it.

What can a man wear on his head and still be heard in the galleries.
How much of the face must show.
Can he eat. Or must he be fed. Being fed may be the joke. Do not decide it tonight.

He is still sure he is the best actor in Athens. The head does not cancel him. It annoys him, which is better.
