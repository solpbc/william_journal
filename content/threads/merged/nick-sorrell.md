# Nick Sorrell's voice

Nick is fifteen, bound to Heminges, the company's leading boy. He has played queens since he was eleven. This year he is the Princess, then the Queen, then Juliet, and the voice those parts need is leaving. William writes Juliet for a voice that may not last until the play is finished. It lasts, managed, until the jig on 28 December, and then it does not.

From world § 1, § 3.3, § 5.1, § 9.2.6 (the casting), and the entries below. He is bound to Heminges, not to Phillips. Phillips trains him. Arthur Meeks is fourteen and waiting. Sam Latchet is twelve and bound to Phillips. Neither takes Juliet. The bean on 6 Jan falls to Nick, not to Sam.

The voice is sound until 26 Sep, when Phillips hears a grain on the Queen's highest line (W084). The crack is 24 Oct, on "Nurse!". It is managed through 27 Dec. It breaks on 28 Dec in the jig after *Errors*. After that he does not sing.

§ 5.2's December ear-line ("I think I am a man now. It is very inconvenient.") is not the speech in S115c. The scene's words are "It's gone, sir" and "It's come, Nick. Sly shall teach thee to fence." Use S115c. Flagged in reasoning.md.
