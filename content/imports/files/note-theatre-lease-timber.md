# Theatre Ground Lease — Figures and Unread Clause

Monday evening, 12 May 1595, at Great St Helen's after playing Navarre.

Cuthbert Burbage came to the house on Saturday with Giles Allen's demand and wanted a sharp word written at Allen. I have never seen Allen and would not curse a man I have not seen.

This afternoon at the Theatre, before the flag went up for *Love's Labour's Lost*, James Burbage put Allen's heads before Heminges, Pope, Richard, and me:

- Rent demanded: twenty-four pounds a year, where the company now pays fourteen.
- Time left on the present lease: two years less a month.
- A clause touching the house itself after five years, which Allen would not explain and none of us has read.

James swore he would pull the Theatre down before he paid a man to read him his own clause. Richard told him to price the timber before he touched a beam. Heminges would not enter a figure none of us had taken, and Pope refused to carry an unread clause into the yard as if it were settled business.

I offered nothing toward a term. I said only that I have a king coming and that it is long — though Saturday's Act 3 leaf still stands half blank on my board, with three words on the tables and nothing added this morning. Nothing was agreed today: not the rent, not the timber, not a word back to Allen.
