# 03 · Places and itinerary: where he was, when, and the gazetteer

**Source book section 3** (request deliverable 3, research area A) · reconciled 2026-11-14 from the three reports in `../`: chatgpt.md (**C**), gemini.md (**G**), claude.md (**Cl**). Tier definitions are in the research request.

**How to read this section**

- **Tiers.** T1 documented · T2 scholarly consensus · T3 contested · T4 tradition or anecdote · T5 imagined. A split such as "T1/T3" means the record is T1 and the reading of it is T3. In the itinerary, the tier is about **him being there**, which is often lower than the tier of the event.
- **Dates** are Julian (Old Style). A record dated January to 24 March "1594" belongs to our 1595 and is written 1594/5. Weekdays use 1 January 1595 = Wednesday; checked against five weekdays the court calendar gives (26 Dec 1594 Thu, 9 Dec 1595 Tue, 17 Nov 1595 Mon, 23 Dec 1595 Tue, 26 Dec 1595 Fri).
- **✔ web** marks a fact checked this session against an outside source (list in § 6). Two reports agreeing is not counted as verification.
- **Coordinates** are WGS84, 4 decimals (about 10 m). The *coord basis* column says where each came from: **ShaLT** = the Shakespearean London Theatres project's published site point; **OSM** = OpenStreetMap geocode of the present-day building or street; **WP** = Wikipedia's site coordinate; **est.** = my estimate from a known neighbouring point, with the error stated. For vanished buildings the point is the best-known site, not the exact footprint.
- **Distances** are computed here (method in § 3.4). Walking times assume 4–5 km/h on crowded, unpaved streets. Steps assume about 1,300 steps per km, a modelling constant (T5): his height and stride are unknown.

## 0. The five things that matter most for writing the journal

1. **One document places him in 1595, and it does not place him anywhere.** The Council warrant "dated at Whitehall" on 15 March 1594/5 (a Saturday) pays Kemp, Shakespeare and Burbage for the court plays of 26 and 28 December 1594 ✔ web. The warrant was drawn up at Whitehall, so it is no evidence he was there. Every other location for him in 1595 is inference, tiered T2 at best.
2. **Home base: St Helen's, Bishopsgate, as a working model (T2/T3), not a fact.** The earliest record placing him in the parish is the default roll of **15 November 1597**, then the subsidy roll of 1 October 1598 ✔ web. Shakespeare Documented says only that he was a householder there "sometime prior to, but no later than, November 15, 1597." Older chronologies put the assessment in October 1596. Marsh (2021) argues for residence from c.1593 (T3). From St Helen's the Theatre is a 16–20 minute walk and the Cross Keys about 6.
3. **The company's year has a documented frame, and his place in it is T2.** It runs: Theatre (and possibly the Cross Keys in winter), with no touring record for 1595 (Chambers: after autumn 1594 the company "does not appear to have travelled" except in 1596 and 1597 ✔ web), then court at **Richmond for Christmas 1595**. Richmond was previously "probable"; it is now confirmed ✔ web. The Chamberlain's Men played there on **26, 27 and 28 December 1595, 6 January and 22 February 1596** (Shrove Sunday), and the payees were Bryan and Heminges, not Shakespeare.
4. **The two open windows for a Stratford trip are Lent and high summer.** Lent ran from Ash Wednesday, 5 March, to Easter, 20 April 1595; the Rose played until 14 March and reopened on 21 April. In summer the Rose was dark from 27 June to 24 August, and the Admiral's Men visited Bath and Maidstone ✔ web. Neither trip is recorded (T3). Either one is a defensible place to put the road journey.
5. **The road is about 100 miles either way, and four days on foot or two to three on horseback.** Via Oxford it is about 102 miles; via Aylesbury and Banbury about 100. I computed both (§ 3.3), and they agree with the reports' 90–100 (C, Cl). The only "documented" inns are wrong for 1595. Aubrey's Crown at Oxford (T4) was taken over by the Davenants c.1600–1603 ✔ web. The Bell in Carter Lane, the Stratford men's London inn, is T1 only for 1598.

### 3.4 How the distances were computed

- **London walks**: routed on OpenStreetMap footpaths (OSRM foot profile, routing.openstreetmap.de) between the site points in § 2. Routes to Southwark were forced through London Bridge's north and south ends. The City's main streets follow their medieval lines, so modern routing is a fair proxy (T2). The vanished lanes and the absence of later bridges are handled by the forced waypoints.
- **River trips**: polylines along the Thames mid-channel, summed by the haversine formula. These are rough, ±10%.
- **Stratford road**: haversine distance between successive towns × **1.12**, a detour factor for pre-turnpike roads. The factor was checked against a known number: Tyburn to Oxford comes out at 57 statute miles, the conventional London–Oxford road figure. Modern car routing along the same towns gives 109–115 miles, which overstates the old road because of bypasses and one-way systems.
- **Times**: walking 4–5 km/h in town, 3 mph on the road; river speeds assume rowing at about 5–6 km/h plus or minus the tide. All are modelling assumptions (T5).
