## 2. What he was plausibly writing in 1595: a working hypothesis by season

**Basis:** consensus dating (T2), sharpened by the NOS best guesses (T3). No document records him writing anything on any day of 1595. Each row below is a hypothesis for the journal, not a fact.

| Season (1595) | Main work in hand | Also in play | Evidence for this placement | Tier |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Jan–Mar** (winter; Lent from 5 Mar) | Finishing and revising ***Love's Labour's Lost*** | The company's two Greenwich comedies of 26 and 28 Dec 1594 (titles unknown); *Errors* after the Gray's Inn "Night of Errors"; ***Love's Labour's Won*** (NOS: early 1595); *Lucrece* and *Venus* selling in Paul's Churchyard | NOS LLL late 1594–early 1595; NOS LLWon early 1595; the court payment collected 15 Mar (T1) | T2 LLL; T3 LLWon |
| **Apr–Jun** (Easter 20 Apr to the June riots) | ***Richard II*** | Reading Holinshed and the new Daniel *Civil Wars*; possibly revising the *Henry VI* plays (NOS) as *True Tragedy* reaches print | NOS R2 mid-1595; Daniel printed 1595 | T2 R2; T3 revisions |
| **Jul–Sep** (riots, martial law, the summer break; Rose dark 27 Jun–24 Aug) | ***Romeo and Juliet***, drafting with Brooke's poem open | sonnets; *Richard II* into rehearsal | NOS R&J late 1595 (range 1593–96); the street-riot texture of 1595 (T5 use) | T2 year; T3 season |
| **Oct–Dec** (autumn and Christmas season) | ***Romeo and Juliet*** finished; ***A Midsummer Night's Dream*** begun | *Richard II* at Hoby's on 9 Dec (T3); *Edward III* entered 1 Dec (T1); court at Christmas | NOS Dream early 1596; Hoby letter 7 Dec | T2/T3 |
| **Into early 1596** | *Dream* finished for 19 Feb 1596 (Carey–Berkeley wedding, T3); *King John* next (NOS mid-1596) | — | NOS | T3 |

**Alternative orders to keep in reserve.**

| Order | Held by | Tier |
|---|---|---|
| R&J first (1594–95), then R2 and Dream (1595–96) | Chambers 1930 | T2 (older) |
| LLL, then R2, R&J and Dream all in 1595, King John 1596 | Oxford 1987; Gemini's Shakespeare Documented summary | T2 |
| R&J as early as 1591 (earthquake line), so a revision in 1595 | a minority | T3 |

**Recommendation for the journal's arc.** LLL (winter) → Richard II (spring) → Romeo and Juliet (summer–autumn) → Dream (from late autumn), with sonnets throughout. It follows the newest chronology, gives each season a different play, and clashes with no T1 fact.

## 3. Working method

| Topic | What we know | Tier | Source (report) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Paper | Bought by the quire (24 sheets) for about 4–5d; folded to folio for a play manuscript | T2 | claude.md |
| Ink and pen | Oak-gall (iron-gall) ink, goose quills, a penknife, sand or pounce to dry the ink, sealing wax | T2 | chatgpt.md, claude.md |
| Light | Tallow candles; wax was for the rich | T2 | claude.md |
| Hand | Secretary script, with italic for names and stage directions; the six signatures and possibly Hand D are the evidence | T1 signatures; T3 Hand D | claude.md |
| Foul papers → fair copy → "book" | The terms are modern scholarly categories inferred from printed texts (chatgpt.md is right to say so). The working model: author's draft, then fair copy, then the licensed playbook with prompter's marks | T2 model; T3 for any given play | all three |
| Licensing | The Master of the Revels, **Edmund Tilney**, read and licensed each playbook, and could cut (his note on *More*: "Leave out ye insurrection wholy"). Office at St John's, Clerkenwell (T2). Henslowe also paid him a playhouse fee: weekly at 5s–6s 8d in 1592; in 1595 the marks "pd" on 31 May and "m[aste]r pd" on 8 Nov and 18 Dec 1595 and 30 Jan 1596, apparently monthly; and on 27 Feb 1596 "the master of the Revelles payd vntell this time al wch J owe hime" (Greg, *Diary* ff. 12v–14v and commentary) | T1 | claude.md; Greg |
| Parts and cue scripts | Each actor got only his own part, his speeches with short cues, copied onto a roll. Edward Alleyn's part of Orlando (*Orlando Furioso*) survives at Dulwich | T1 Alleyn part; T2 practice | chatgpt.md, claude.md |
| Plots | A backstage "plot" hung up to list entrances. Dulwich has examples (e.g. *2 Seven Deadly Sins*, from before 1594, naming Strange's players who became Chamberlain's Men) | T1 | claude.md; Greg |
| Rehearsal | Short group rehearsals; actors studied their parts alone (Tiffany Stern, *Rehearsal from Shakespeare to Sheridan*, 2000). Morning rehearsal and afternoon performance is a plausible rhythm, but no hours are recorded for any 1595 day | T2 | chatgpt.md, claude.md |
| How a new play entered the repertory | Henslowe shows the pattern: a "ne" premiere at a raised take (often £3+), then 8–16 more performances spread over months, never on consecutive days except two-part plays (e.g. 1 and 2 *Hercules* on 12–13 Jun 1595). New plays arrived about every two to four weeks (16 in 1595, § 5) | T1 (Admiral's); T2 by analogy for his company | Greg; claude.md |
| His output | About two plays a year as company dramatist | T2 | claude.md |
| Writers' pay (analogue) | Henslowe paid about £6 a play in 1597–1600 | T1 for the Admiral's Men; T2 by analogy | claude.md |
| Print | Plays sold as small quartos of about 9–12 sheets, unbound, about 6d. Title pages advertised the company. His name first appears on a play title page in 1598 | T2; T1 for the 1598 title pages | claude.md |

## 5. The rival repertory: the Admiral's Men at the Rose

**What Henslowe's "diary" is.** An account book (Dulwich College, MSS 7), not a private diary. For each playing day it gives the date, the play and Henslowe's receipt, with "ne" beside new plays (T1). Images: [Henslowe-Alleyn Digitisation Project](https://henslowe-alleyn.org.uk/essays/henslowes-diary-1591-1609/). Transcription: Greg 1904, vol. 1 ([archive.org](https://archive.org/details/cu31924026121305)); commentary, vol. 2 ([archive.org](https://archive.org/details/cu31924026121313)); the standard modern edition is Foakes (2nd ed., 2002). Each play's history: [Lost Plays Database](https://lostplays.folger.edu/).

**The dating traps.**

1. **The year.** Henslowe writes "1594" until Lady Day, so his "Jan–Mar 1594" is our 1595, and his "16 Jan 1595" (*Pythagoras*) is **1596** (chatgpt.md, correct).
2. **The day numbers slip.** At Easter 1595 Henslowe skips the 22nd, writes 23–31 April (there is no 31 April) and is back in step on 1 May. On the real calendar the week runs: **Mon 21 Apr** *French Doctor* · **Tue 22** *Knack* · **Wed 23** *Grecian Comedy* · **Thu 24** *Wise Man* · **Fri 25** *Wise Man* (£3) · **Sat 26** *Godfrey* · **Mon 28** *Warlamchester* · **Tue 29** *Long Meg* · **Wed 30** *Faustus* · **Thu 1 May** *Long Meg* (50s). This realignment is claude.md's, confirmed by the editor from Easter and the six-day week (T2 inference on a T1 record). Similar slips occur in Jan, Feb, May and Sep–Dec 1595, flagged ⚠ below.
3. **The Sundays.** The Admiral's did not normally play on Sundays, so the 11 dates that compute to a Sunday are almost certainly slips, not Sunday performances.
