# Whitehall Warrant Reckoning — Personal Note

Saturday afternoon, 15 March 1595, back at Great St Helen's from James Burbage's house in Holywell.

The Treasurer of the Chamber paid the twenty pounds in coin at Whitehall this morning against the warrant for St Stephen's Day and Innocents' Day at Greenwich: thirteen pounds six shillings and eightpence for the two comedies, and six pounds thirteen shillings and fourpence by way of Her Majesty's reward — sixteen angels, four sovereigns, and eight pounds in white money.

John Heminges cast the sheet at Holywell before dinner:

- Sixteen shillings for boat-hire to Greenwich and back across the two days with the costume baskets.
- Thirty-four shillings in rewards to five hired men, three boys, the book-keeper, and the tireman.
- Fifty shillings to the mercer in Cheapside for cloth taken up before Christmas.
- Fifteen pounds divided into eight equal parts — seven sharers and one part to the company stock — thirty-seven shillings and sixpence apiece. Out of the stock part Heminges set aside eight shillings for Mistress Edith Kettlewood, payable Thursday.

Richard Burbage asked for seven yards of peach-coloured velvet in grain for a king. Heminges put the velvet off till autumn, and no one pressed him.

I told them at the table that my thirty-seven shillings and sixpence goes toward a hired horse for Warwickshire in Lent. Home now, I have laid the coin by in the chest without writing a second line against it. The road north is near enough without spending the purse twice.
