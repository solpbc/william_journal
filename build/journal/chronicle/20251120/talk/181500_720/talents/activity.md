At supper the loaf was two fingers short of the notch, and a penny. You asked Verbeke what a weaver is proudest of. He said the shuttle thrown true, then the beam, the reed, and the thrum, the waste end on the loom. The core you wind the thread on is a bottom, a bottom of thread, and a man who winds well is half a weaver. He tied the weaver's knot twice on packthread and made you tie it. He gave you three words of his own tongue, for bread, for thread, and for good evening. You did not write the letters. July's three were thread, the shuttle, and the knot, and these were not those. Mistress Ansell was told a duke's wedding, tradesmen who make a play, and fairies in the wood. She called it a satire upon players and approved it, and she called the fairies popery. Alse asked if the weaver is in the play. You said he wants to be all of it. There is no page.
