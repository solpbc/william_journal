At Old Swan, Davy Gannet said Drake and old Hawkins were out of Plymouth, six-and-thirty sail and four thousand men, gone to burn the King of Spain in his bed. It is seven days old. They sailed on Thursday the twenty-eighth of August. Bryan set the merchants' letters against him: seven-and-twenty ships, and some five-and-twenty hundred men, Gannet out by his usual third. Gannet said, lower, that two old sea-dogs in one command never yet made easy sailing. Nobody on the stair could say where they were bound. A man on the top step said it was the answer for Penzance. Bryan said that was what the ballad would say, and that nobody had told him so. The ballad-seller had a ship that has served for three other voyages. You did not buy it. You thought of five-and-twenty hundred men who went aboard in a dearth year because a ship feeds a man, and you said only that it would be spring before we know.
