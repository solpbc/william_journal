# Vial Scene — Cues, Copy, and Chamber Notes

Wednesday night, 22 October 1595, at Great St Helen's after laying the loose leaf aside.

Morning business at the Theatre is settled. Humphrey Larkham laid out Gaunt's roll, York's, and the Queen's where Abel Garnish's shortened cue clashed with my own; John Heminges held to paper first, then mouths, and we backed eightpence out of the company stock entered as copying. Humphrey marked all three corrected rolls with the small sign he and Abel settled in the winter, and Nick Sorrell spoke the new cue once.

Tonight by the short candle I set down two lines of Juliet's chamber speech and stopped at *Nurse!* — not letting her drink tonight. The loose leaf lies apart from the red-tied bundle on the board. Better to leave the draught unpoured than hurry the fear out of it.

Working reminders before the scene goes to copy:

- Keep the line pitch where Juliet sits; do not drop the measure to spare Nick's throat. The grain he has carried since late September is still holding, and writing her low would flatten the fear.
- No margin prompts telling the boy how to feel the line. Writing *weep here* earlier this month only made him stiffen on the word. Let the pace of the questions carry the terror.
- Chamber properties to keep plain when the scene comes to Garnish: one small glass phial, one wax taper, and the curtained bed. Nothing else on the boards to distract from her hand.
