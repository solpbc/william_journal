# Summer Reckoning and Vote — 7 July

Monday night, 7 July 1595, back at Great St Helen's after Richard's supper at the Jackdaw.

After a half yard for *Love's Labour's Lost*, we shut the tiring-house door and sat on upturned chests — James and Richard Burbage, Heminges, Kemp, George Bryan, Thomas Pope, Augustine Phillips, and myself — to settle whether to take a wagon onto the road or hold the Theatre.

What Heminges set down and carried:

- Play on in Shoreditch through Saturday, 26 July (St James's tide).
- Rest the house in August and re-open for Bartholomew Fair, Monday 25 August.
- Look at the question again on Thursday, 17 July, before any money is set under a road.
- Vote: carried, Kemp standing alone against.

Kemp wanted the country road — Kent, he guessed, where they have not heard him yet — while Bryan put a constable and my lord's warrant on every mile between here and a town none of us can name. Heminges laid out the cost plainly: oats and horse-hire dearer every mile, country mayors who dread a crowd worse than ours does, five shillings a week for hired men on the road against ten here, and a wagon eating coin every day it moves where an empty yard charges us only on the days we open it. James took the twenty-sixth to keep his house earning; Richard asked for three clear weeks in August to con the King; Augustine sat his chest and kept silent.

Thomas Pope forced me to say what I knew was true: a quiet August suits me better than it suits the hired men. I am not proud of the fit, and I voted with John. The month is for the pages, and the pages are not a wage. Heminges entered the three dates at the foot of his sheet with no sum under them. Later at the Jackdaw a capon went between four, Richard took the pearwood glass, Augustine sang, and the jaw made me leave at nine.
