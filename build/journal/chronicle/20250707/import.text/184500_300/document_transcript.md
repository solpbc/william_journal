# Company Summer Reckoning Memorandum — July 1595

Prepared by John Heminges for the sharers of the Lord Chamberlain's Men at the Theatre, Shoreditch, Monday 7 July 1595.

Present: James Burbage, Richard Burbage, John Heminges, William Kempe, George Bryan, Thomas Pope, Augustine Phillips, William Shakespeare.

## Part 1: Receipts and Yard Standing

Following the June stirs and the Council's letters, afternoon gatherings at the Theatre have thinned to half a yard, though the Bankside has nowhere else of an afternoon to spend a penny while the river stays closed to us. An empty yard charges us only on the days we open it, whereas a wagon on the country road costs us every day it moves.

The company stock holds sufficient coin to carry July playdays through St James's tide without borrowing against the winter court season, provided no carriage is hired before the Council's mind is settled.

## Part 2: Travelling Charges and Hired Men

Road expenses weighed against London openings:

1. Oats and horse-hire stand dearer every mile in a summer like this, before a single town bailiff has been approached.
2. Country mayors dread a crowd worse than the City does; players on the road must show my lord's warrant to each constable in turn before a scaffold goes up.
3. Hired men take five shillings a week on the country road against ten shillings in London, while every day between towns eats provender and returns nothing to the boxes.

No road destination is named on this sheet, and no carriage sum is written while the road stays a guess.

## Part 3: Resolution and Dates

The company resolves:

- Last London playing day before rest: Saturday, 26 July 1595 (St James's tide).
- House re-opens in Shoreditch: Monday, 25 August 1595 (Bartholomew Fair).
- Re-check date: Thursday, 17 July 1595, to weigh the City's temper and the boxes before setting any money under a road.

Vote entered: Carried, Kemp against. No sum set under the dates.
