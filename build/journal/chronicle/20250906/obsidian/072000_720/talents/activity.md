Later in the morning you wrote the how. The wound is under the boy's own arm, the boy coming between, and it is hardly a wound. You set down that it is not so deep as a well, nor so wide as a church door, but it is enough, and that they should ask tomorrow and find him a grave man. You gave him eight lines more and struck all eight in one stroke. The curse in this setting-down is the short one. You thought once of Kit Marlowe, dead over a reckoning at Deptford two summers since, and you put the thought away without writing the name. You made no other blot. The rest of the play has to go on without the talker.
