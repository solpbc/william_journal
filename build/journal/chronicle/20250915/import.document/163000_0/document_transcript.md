## Page 1

PETITION OF THE LORD MAYOR AND ALDERMEN OF LONDON TO
THE PRIVY COUNCIL
Guildhall copyist’s digest of the letter of 13 September 1595 (received in
Shoreditch, 15 September)
To the Right Honourable the Lords and others of Her Majesty’s Most Honourable
Privy Council. The humble suit of the Lord Mayor and Aldermen of the City of
London touching stage plays in the suburbs and liberties adjoining.
Heads reported from the Guildhall letter of 13 September:
1. Disorders and Resort. The petition names the Bankside playhouses, cites the
late June stirs and disorderly assemblies of apprentices, vagrants, and
masterless men, and prays letters to the Justices of Surrey and Middlesex.
Whether the Theatre in Shoreditch is named in so many words the Guildhall
copyist could not swear.
2. Contagion and Scarcity. The City pleads the dearness of corn and danger of
infection from crowds drawn out of the parishes into the liberties on working
afternoons.
3. Prayer for Restraint. The Aldermen pray the Council to grant letters of
restraint forbidding stage plays in and about the suburbs until the City’s
quiet be better assured.
Note endorsed at the Theatre, Monday 15 September 1595:
No Council order has come down. The afternoon bill for The Knave stood as
posted at two of the clock; the company awaits word from the Lord Chamberlain
before altering any playing day.
