To my welbeloued wyf Anne Shakespeare,
at the house in Henley streete, Stratford,
these.
From London this Candlemas day, 1594.

Anne. I am mended of the cold. The comedy
is done. Of Lent, more when I know the day.

Hamnet. Thou art ten. Mind Master
Aspinall's Latin. Do not mend the
fastening of this packet before the
things are out of it. Write me whether
a boy of ten can now outrun a boy of
nine.

Iudith. The same grote, and the same
order. A riddle is in the paper.
Susanna must read it thee, and then
keep it from thee. Do not understand
"equall" so cleverly that thy sister
has to judge between you. A grote
apiece is equall. The spending is thine.

Susanna. Two lines, so thou art not
left out, and my thanks for the shoes.
The riddle is: what is bought by the
pound and burned by the inch? Keep
the answer. It is a candle. Tell them
the comfits are to be eaten, not saved
for a better day.

A grote each, to be spent foolishly.
A twist of comfits in the paper.
God keep you all.

        Thy husband, W. S.
